> **The Answer in 60 Seconds**
>
> Commercial drone (Unmanned Aircraft, UA) operations in Singapore are regulated by the [Civil Aviation Authority of Singapore (CAAS)](https://www.caas.gov.sg/) under the Air Navigation Act 1966 and the [Air Navigation (101 Unmanned Aircraft Operations) Regulations 2019](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/SL/ANA1966-S833-2019). Operators flying for **business or any other non-recreational, non-educational purpose** need an Unmanned Aircraft Pilot Licence (UAPL) whatever the UA's weight, as do recreational or educational pilots of UA over 7kg; business operations also need a UA Operator Permit and a Class 1 Activity Permit. Singapore Public Liability wordings such as MSIG's SUMO and AIG's Commercial General Liability wording **exclude aircraft**, in terms that reach drones, so operators need a specific UA Liability extension or a standalone aviation policy. Typical cover stack: **UA Liability** (third-party injury and property damage), **Hull cover** (the drone equipment itself), **Care/Custody/Control** (client property being filmed), and **Professional Indemnity** (the photographic output and creative services).

### The Sourced Detail

Drone aerial photography has become a Singapore SME niche of meaningful scale - wedding videographers, real estate marketing, construction progress documentation, event coverage, and inspection services all routinely use UA. A general PL policy can leave these businesses without cover for drone use, because wordings such as MSIG's SUMO and AIG's CGL exclude aircraft.

#### The CAAS regulatory framework

Per [CAAS's Unmanned Aircraft (UA) regulatory framework](https://www.caas.gov.sg/public-passengers/unmanned-aircraft), operators must comply with several layers of regulation:

1. **Registration.** UA above the de minimis weight threshold must be registered with CAAS. Verify the current threshold and registration process directly on the CAAS website before operating.

2. **Pilot licensing.** An Unmanned Aircraft Pilot Licence (UAPL) is required for business or other non-recreational, non-educational flying whatever the UA's weight, and for recreational or educational flying of UA over 7kg. It is issued after the pilot passes a CAAS theory test and a practical assessment by an Authorised Flight Examiner from a CAAS-approved UA Training and Assessment Organisation; the training course itself is optional.

3. **Operator Permit.** Businesses operating UA require a UA Operator Permit, which CAAS grants after assessing the applicant's organisational setup, safety procedures including risk assessments, the airworthiness of each UA and the competency of the people flying it.

4. **Activity Permits.** Every business UA activity also needs a Class 1 Activity Permit, which is valid only with an Operator Permit and is granted for a single activity or a block of repeated activities after CAAS assesses the location, type of operation, schedule, operating altitude and risk mitigation. Flying in a protected area also needs a permit from the Singapore Police Force.

5. **Operational rules.** Altitude limits, distance from aerodromes and no-fly zones are set out in the Air Navigation (101 Unmanned Aircraft Operations) Regulations 2019 and CAAS advisory circulars.

6. **No-fly zones.** Restricted areas around Changi Airport, Seletar Airport, Paya Lebar Airbase, military installations, key infrastructure. Check them on [OneMap](https://www.onemap.gov.sg/droneQuery), which CAAS calls the only authoritative source for no-fly zones.

Penalties for non-compliance include fines and imprisonment under the Air Navigation Act 1966. Insurance does not cover criminal penalties.

#### Why standard PL policies don't cover drones

Singapore Public Liability wordings checked for this guide exclude aircraft. The MSIG SUMO Public Liability section, for example, excludes liability arising from:

> "the ownership possession or use by or on behalf of the Insured of any watercraft aircraft or thing made or intended to float on or in or travel on or through water air or space."

In the two Singapore wordings checked, the aircraft exclusion sets no size limit: AIG's CGL wording defines Aircraft as a "Vehicle, vessel or thing designed to be used primarily in the air and to be supported by the dynamic reaction of the air upon the Vehicle's, vessel's or thing's wings or rotor-blades", and the SUMO exclusion covers any "thing made or intended to float on or in or travel on or through water air or space". Some policies have a specific drone exclusion separately.

The result: under wordings like these, a wedding photographer operating a drone has *no* third-party liability cover under a general PL policy if the drone causes injury or property damage. They need either:
- A UA Liability extension to the PL policy, where the insurer offers one
- A standalone Aviation Liability / UA policy

#### What UA Liability cover actually responds to

A typical UA Liability policy covers:

- **Third-party bodily injury** caused by the UA - the drone collides with someone, falls and injures someone, propeller injury during launch/recovery
- **Third-party property damage** - drone crashes into a building, vehicle, equipment, structure
- **Legal defence costs** for claims against the operator
- **Limits of indemnity** as set in the policy schedule

What it doesn't cover:
- The drone itself (separate Hull cover required)
- Property in the operator's care/custody/control (e.g. a client's roof being filmed if it's actively being worked on - separate CCC extension)
- Professional output errors (separate PI required)
- War, terrorism, malicious acts (standard exclusions)
- Operations outside the policy territory or activity scope
- Operations in breach of CAAS rules or Activity Permit requirements

#### Hull cover - the drone equipment itself

Commercial drones range from prosumer models to professional cinematography drones and specialist inspection drones with thermal or LIDAR payloads, and their replacement values differ widely.

Hull cover responds to:
- Accidental damage during operation (crash, hard landing, propeller strike)
- Theft (subject to security warranties)
- Damage in transit (subject to transit warranties)
- Sometimes: damage during shipping, manufacturer defect (often excluded)

#### Care, Custody and Control (CCC) - the often-missed cover

If a drone operator is filming a client's property - a developer's construction site, a homeowner's roof, an event venue's interior - and damages that property during operations, the standard UA Liability policy may exclude the loss as "property in the insured's care, custody, or control."

A CCC extension or separate Bailee cover responds.

#### Professional Indemnity - for the creative output

A drone aerial photographer is also a professional service provider. Errors in the output - wrong angles, missed shots, corrupted footage, failure to deliver to brief - can give rise to professional liability claims. A PI policy covers:

- Negligent advice or service delivery
- Breach of professional duty
- Errors in deliverables

#### Insurance requirements often imposed by clients and venues

Beyond CAAS regulatory requirements, commercial drone operators frequently encounter contractual insurance requirements:

- **Wedding venues** may ask for evidence of liability cover, sometimes with the venue named as additional insured, before allowing drone operations
- **Construction sites** may impose insurance requirements through the subcontract, such as naming the main contractor as additional insured
- **Real estate marketing** clients (developers, agents) often require evidence of cover before commissioning aerial work
- **Event organisers** often require evidence of cover plus Activity Permit verification

Operators without the right cover lose commercial work, not just to compliance - clients reject operators who cannot produce a Certificate of Insurance.

### Common Mistakes / What Goes Wrong

1. **Assuming general PL covers drone operations.** Wordings such as MSIG's SUMO and AIG's CGL exclude aircraft, in terms that reach drones.
2. **Operating without a UAPL or Operator Permit.** Beyond the regulatory penalty, a policy that excludes operations in breach of CAAS requirements would not respond.
3. **Flying in restricted airspace without an Activity Permit.** Where the policy excludes operations in breach of CAAS rules, there is no cover for that operation.
4. **No Hull cover on a S$10,000+ drone.** A propeller strike on a windy day is a write-off; without Hull, the operator absorbs the full loss.
5. **No CCC extension when filming client property.** Damage to the building being filmed is often excluded under standard UA Liability.
6. **No PI for service delivery.** A client who alleges the operator missed agreed deliverables can claim against PI; without it, the operator faces personal exposure.
7. **Single-job cover when multiple jobs are run weekly.** Some operators buy event-specific cover; for active commercial operators, an annual policy is far more economical and continuous.
8. **Hobbyist policies misapplied to commercial operations.** Some hobbyist drone insurance products are non-commercial only; using them for paid work voids cover.

### What This Means for Your Business

For a Singapore commercial drone operator, the typical cover stack:

1. **CAAS UAPL** for the pilot, **UA Operator Permit** for the business, and a **Class 1 Activity Permit** for each activity or block of repeated activities.

2. **UA Liability** with a limit appropriate to the work and to any minimum the client or venue sets.

3. **Hull cover** on each drone in the fleet, including spares and payloads (cameras, gimbals, sensors). Consider in-transit cover if drones travel between sites.

4. **CCC extension** for client property exposure during operations.

5. **Professional Indemnity** if delivering creative or technical services.

6. **WICA** if employing pilots, ground crew, or assistants. Single-pilot owner-operators are not employees of themselves. WICA covers employees under a contract of service or apprenticeship, not independent contractors or the self-employed, and WIC insurance is required for all employees doing manual work and for non-manual employees whose salary, not counting overtime, bonuses, incentive payments and allowances, is S$2,600 a month or less.

7. **Cyber** if storing client footage and personal data - particularly relevant for real estate work involving recognisable people, addresses, and property details.

Whatever the premium, the asymmetry of an uninsured drone strike against a moving vehicle on a public road, or a runaway drone landing in a wedding crowd, is total.

For operators considering scaling up, the insurance build is a moat: clients who require Certificates of Insurance, additional insured endorsements, and waiver of subrogation cannot work with operators who cannot produce them. Compliant operators win the work; non-compliant operators stay informal.

### Questions to Ask Your Adviser

1. Does my UA Liability policy cover all the specific operations I conduct - events, construction, BVLOS, night flying - or only standard daylight operations?
2. Is the limit of indemnity appropriate for my client mix, including any contractual minimums imposed by clients or venues?
3. Is Hull cover on a stated value or market value basis, and does it cover transit, storage, and accidental damage during operations?
4. Is the Care/Custody/Control extension included, and what is the sub-limit?
5. Do I need separate Professional Indemnity for the creative/technical service component, and how does it interact with UA Liability for output disputes?
