> **The Answer in 60 Seconds**
>
> The [BCA Contractors Registration System (CRS)](https://www1.bca.gov.sg/procurement/pre-tender-stage/contractors-registration-system-crs) is the registry through which contractors qualify to tender for public-sector construction work and (from 1 June 2025) to hire foreign construction workers (Work Permit and S Pass holders). The most consequential 2024-2026 change for Singapore construction SMEs is the 5 March 2024 announcement that, from 1 June 2025, CRS becomes a nation-wide registry: all firms hiring foreign construction workers, regardless of public-sector or private-sector activity, must be CRS-registered. Minimum track records and paid-up capital thresholds were raised by the 7 June 2024 BCA circular. The current CRS Guidelines (September 2025) and per-group Specific Registration Requirements (CW SRR and CR SRR, both June 2025 editions) are the authoritative documents. The CRS has five registration groups with about 50 workheads: Construction Workhead (CW), Construction-Related Workhead (CR), Mechanical and Electrical Workhead (ME), Trade Heads (TR) and Regulatory Workhead (RW). Facilities management (FM) and supply (SY) firms register in two separate BCA registries, not in the CRS. Financial grading varies by group: CW has 7 grades (C3 to A1), CR and ME have 6 grades (L1 to L6) with some CR workheads single grade, and TR and RW are single grade. The CRS itself sets no insurance requirement. Three things bear on the insurance a construction SME carries: WIC insurance, which WICA 2019 requires; the safety duties under the Workplace Safety and Health (Construction) Regulations, which set no insurance requirement but govern the site risks the covers respond to; and each project's contract, which sets the CAR, public liability and any professional indemnity the job needs.

### The Sourced Detail

The BCA Contractors Registration System is the nation-wide registry of construction firms that BCA administers. Historically, CRS was the qualification register for public-sector construction tenders. From 1 June 2025, the system's scope expanded substantively: every firm hiring foreign construction workers, regardless of whether it bids for public-sector work, must be CRS-registered.

#### What changed and why it matters

**5 March 2024 announcement and 1 June 2025 expansion.** BCA announced that from 1 June 2025, CRS would become a nation-wide registry. The substantive effect: a private-sector sub-contractor SME with foreign Work Permit or S Pass construction workers, historically outside the CRS perimeter, must be CRS-registered before it applies for a new work pass or renews an existing one for a foreign construction worker; workers it already employed could be kept until their passes expired. What registering first means for the work-permit application, and for the medical insurance that follows each worker, the work injury insurance required for each manual worker and the security bond for each non-Malaysian Work Permit holder, is in [CRS registration before hiring Work Permit holders](/regulatory-change/bca-crs-expansion-national-registry-1-june-2025).

The policy rationale, as BCA put it, is that a nation-wide registry lets it implement quality, manpower, productivity and innovation measures across the construction industry more effectively. Before the change, firms could reach foreign construction workers through three gateways (the CRS, BCA's Builders Licensing Scheme and SCAL's Singapore List of Trade Sub-contractors) with different entry requirements, and some firms went through none of them. BCA said the change would apply the same minimum standards to all firms hiring foreign construction workers.

**7 June 2024 circular: track record and paid-up capital thresholds raised.** BCA's circular set out the revised minimums for track record (TR) and paid-up capital (PUC) across workheads. The increase applied at the entry grades (C3, L1 and Single Grade) from 1 June 2025: minimum PUC rose to S$50,000 and minimum TR to S$300,000 over the past three years, for all categories except CR10A. The circular did not change the requirements for higher grades. The current TR and PUC minimums for each workhead and grade are in the Specific Registration Requirements (SRR) for each registration group, linked from the [BCA CRS page](https://www1.bca.gov.sg/procurement/pre-tender-stage/contractors-registration-system-crs); the CRS Guidelines (as of September 2025) set out the general rules.

**Specialist Diploma in Construction Productivity (SDCP) requirements.** In CW, grades A1, A2 and B1 need at least one technical person holding the Specialist Diploma in Construction Productivity (SDCP) or the Certified Construction Productivity Professional (CCPP) certification. At L6, the graded CR workheads need one professional with SDCP, and ME workheads ME01 to ME13 need one SDCP holder except ME07 and ME13. RP, P and T are BCA's personnel classes: Registerable Professional, Professional and Technician.

#### The workhead taxonomy

What each workhead covers is in [BCA CRS workheads and financial grades explained](/association/bca-crs-workheads-overview).

The CRS Guidelines (as of September 2025) set out five registration groups: CW, CR, ME, TR and RW. FM and SY, shown below, are separate BCA registries, not CRS groups:

**Construction Workhead (CW).** Building and civil engineering main contractor categories. Includes CW01 (General Building) and CW02 (Civil Engineering). Financial grades: C3, C2, C1, B2, B1, A2, A1 (seven grades).

**Construction-Related Workhead (CR).** Construction-related specialist works. Includes structural and geotechnical specialist categories such as piling (CR08), ground support and stabilisation (CR12) and precast concrete production (CR10A, CR10B); mechanical and electrical work sits under ME, not CR. Financial grades: L1, L2, L3, L4, L5, L6 (six grades). Some workheads (CR01, CR03, CR10A, CR15, CR17, CR18) have single grading.

**Mechanical and Electrical Workhead (ME).** M&E specialist contractor categories. Financial grades: L1, L2, L3, L4, L5, L6.

**Facilities Management Workhead (FM).** FM01 has 4 grades (M1, M2, M3, M4). FM02-04 have 6 grades (L1 to L6).

**Supply Head (SY).** Supply of construction materials and equipment. Financial grades: L1 to L6.

**Trade Head (TR).** Specialised trades. Single grading.

**Regulatory Workhead (RW).** Regulatory specialist categories. Single grading.

Tendering limits per workhead and grade are valid for one year from 1 July to 30 June and are re-tabulated annually at the BCA tendering-limits page. SMEs and brokers should verify current tendering limits at the [CRS Tendering Limits page](https://www1.bca.gov.sg/procurement/pre-tender-stage/contractors-registration-system-crs/crs-tendering-limits). For the CW01 and CW02 figures grade by grade, see [CW01 and CW02 tendering limits by grade](/licensing/bca-cw01-cw02-contractor-insurance-requirements).

Sample minimum paid-up capital values from the current CR SRR (June 2025 edition): every L1 and single-grade CR workhead requires S$50,000, except CR10A (automated precast concrete production), which requires S$2 million. L2 also requires S$50,000; from L3 the minimum rises with the grade, to S$1.5 million at L6.

#### Insurance interaction with CRS

The CRS sets no insurance requirement of its own. Three channels bear on the insurance an SME carries:

**Channel 1: WIC insurance under WICA 2019.** Every construction SME with manual employees or non-manual employees whose salary, not counting overtime, bonuses, the annual wage supplement, incentive payments and allowances, is S$2,600 per month or less must hold WIC insurance from an MOM Designated Insurer (see [how insurers win and lose that status](/regulatory-change/mom-designated-insurer-mechanics-wica-2019)). The compensation limit uplift on 1 November 2025 (death S$269,000 maximum; permanent incapacity S$346,000 maximum; medical S$53,000) automatically applies to WIC insurance policies. When the policy is confirmed, the employer gives the designated insurer its UEN, nature of business, total headcount, total annual wages and the occupations of its employees, and MOM warns that under-declaring the number of employees may affect all claims.

**Channel 2: Workplace Safety and Health (Construction) Regulations.** The [Workplace Safety and Health Act 2006](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/WSHA2006) and the WSH (Construction) Regulations 2007 (see [what changed for sub-contractors in 2024-2026](/regulatory-change/wsh-construction-regulations-sub-contractor-sme-2024-2026)) set safety duties, not insurance requirements; they govern the site risks that CAR, public liability and WIC cover respond to. BizSAFE, the Workplace Safety and Health Council's programme, recognises at Level 3 and above that a firm has systems to manage workplace risks and complies with the WSH (Risk Management) Regulations. BizSAFE Level 3 or ISO 45001 is itself a CRS registration requirement at some grades and workheads, for example CW grades C1 and C2. At CW grades B2 and above, ISO 45001 or BizSAFE Star is a CRS registration requirement.

**Channel 3: Project-level contract requirements.** Public-sector projects use the [Public Sector Standard Conditions of Contract (PSSCOC)](https://www1.bca.gov.sg/procurement/post-tender-stage/public-sector-standard-conditions-of-contract-psscoc), which require the works to be insured for not less than the contract sum and leave the limits of third-party liability insurance to each contract. Private-sector main contracts set their own insurance requirements. The covers a contract can ask for:

Contractors All Risks (CAR) for not less than the contract sum; under the PSSCOC the works policy is in the joint names of the employer and the contractor (clause 28.1).

Public Liability at the limits the contract sets (under the PSSCOC, the limit for any one accident for third-party property damage is stated in the Appendix against clause 27.1(1)(b)), with the employer noted as an additional insured with a cross liability provision.

Professional Indemnity where the contract asks for it; the PSSCOC for Design and Build 2020 has no professional indemnity insurance clause of its own.

Work injury compensation: the WIC insurance in Channel 1.

Procurement implications: a higher CRS grade raises the value of public-sector contracts a firm can tender for, and a larger contract can set higher insurance limits.

#### Claim-time worked example

SME B operates as CW02 B1 (civil engineering, B1 grade). It wins a private-sector civil engineering subcontract worth S$22 million.

Main contract requirements:
- Contractors All Risks (CAR) for full contract value (S$22 million).
- Public Liability of S$5 million any one occurrence.
- Certified WICA cover.
- BizSafe Level 3 minimum.

SME B's existing programme:
- WIC insurance: in place with an MOM Designated Insurer.
- Public Liability: S$2 million any one occurrence (below the contract requirement).
- CAR: project-specific (procured by the main contractor in joint names; SME B verifies inclusion).
- BizSafe Level 3: in place.

The procurement workflow:
- PL uplift required from S$2 million to S$5 million any one occurrence. SME B engages the broker; underwriter requires loss runs, signed work statements, and a site-safety plan referencing the BCA prevailing standards.
- Premium uplift is meaningful. The broker tests two alternative carriers; the more competitive quote is selected.
- Concurrently from 1 June 2025: SME B must be CRS-registered before it applies for or renews a work pass for a foreign construction worker. As a CW02 B1 firm it is already registered; the 1 June 2025 increase in minimum TR and PUC applied to the entry grades only, so the B1 thresholds (S$3 million PUC and S$30 million TR over three years) did not change.
- SME B verifies its named-Insured status on the CAR schedule, the cross-liability clause in PL, and the waiver of subrogation against fellow Insureds.

Subsequent renewal alignment:
- Annual WIC insurance renewal aligned with the 1 November 2025 compensation limit uplift.
- CAR renewal at project end-of-section milestones.
- PL renewal at the SME's standard renewal date, with sum-insured retained at S$5 million given ongoing contract exposure.

### Common Mistakes / What Goes Wrong

1. **Not registering for CRS before applying for or renewing a construction work pass.** From 1 June 2025, a firm operating only in private-sector projects must be CRS-registered before it applies for a new work pass or renews an existing one for a foreign construction worker. Workers it already employed could be kept until their passes expired.

2. **Buying Public Liability cover at low limit without checking project contract requirements.** The PSSCOC does not fix a Public Liability limit; each contract sets its own, and it can be higher than the limit on a standard SME package.

3. **Assuming the main contractor's CAR cover automatically extends to sub-contractors.** CAR policies in joint names list specifically named Insureds. Sub-contractors should verify inclusion on the policy schedule and the cross-liability and waiver-of-subrogation clauses.

4. **Not holding the safety certification a CRS grade requires.** BizSAFE Level 3 or ISO 45001 is a CRS registration requirement at CW grades C1 and C2, and ISO 45001 or BizSAFE Star at CW grades B2 and above, so a firm below those levels cannot register at those grades.

5. **Not refreshing TR and PUC at the threshold uplift announcement.** The 7 June 2024 circular raised minimums; SMEs at the prior threshold may need to demonstrate enhanced TR or PUC at renewal of CRS registration.

6. **Treating WIC insurance and EFMA medical liability as synonymous.** EFMA imposes a statutory medical-expenses liability for foreign workers that can exceed the WICA medical cap of S$53,000. Any medical cost above what the employer's WIC and medical insurance pay can remain the employer's to bear under the work pass conditions.

7. **Inflating track records or paid-up capital to obtain higher CRS grades.** False information in a CRS application is a ground for BCA to suspend or de-register a firm under the CRS Terms of Registration, and it can also lead to criminal charges: in November 2022 individuals were charged with cheating for allegedly using false documents to obtain a C1 grading from BCA.

8. **Using unlicensed or unregistered sub-contractors.** From 1 June 2025, a sub-contractor must be CRS-registered before it applies for or renews a work pass for a foreign construction worker. On public-sector projects, first-level sub-contractors must themselves be registered in the CRS.

9. **Missing the SDCP requirement at A1-B1 (CW) or L6 (CR, ME) grades.** At these grades at least one technical person must hold SDCP (in CW, SDCP or CCPP; in ME, the rule covers ME01 to ME13 except ME07 and ME13). Failure to meet the SDCP requirement blocks grade qualification.

10. **Not checking insurance limits after a CRS grade upgrade.** A higher grade raises the value of public-sector contracts a firm can tender for, and each contract sets its own insurance limits.

### What This Means for Your Business

For a Singapore construction SME, the structural priority is CRS registration alignment with the 1 June 2025 expansion. SMEs operating only in private-sector projects but employing foreign Work Permit or S Pass construction workers must be CRS-registered before they apply for or renew a work pass for a foreign construction worker. The eBACS portal is the application channel; the CRS Guidelines (as of September 2025) set the general rules, and each group's Specific Registration Requirements set the requirements for each workhead and grade.

For an SME upgrading CRS grade to tender for larger public-sector contracts, the insurance programme has to meet each larger contract's terms. A larger contract means a larger CAR sum insured (under the PSSCOC, not less than the contract sum) and whatever Public Liability limit that contract sets; in CW, a higher grade also needs BizSAFE Level 3 or ISO 45001 at C1 and C2, and BizSAFE Star or ISO 45001 from B2 up.

For an SME at the threshold of a grade uplift, the higher grade brings its own registration requirements, which can include more paid-up capital and net worth, a larger track record, more technical personnel and, at some grades, a safety certification or an SDCP holder (in CW, SDCP or CCPP). The CRS sets no insurance requirement; the CAR, public liability and any professional indemnity are set by each contract the firm wins.

### Questions to Ask Your Adviser

1. Is our firm CRS-registered, and which workhead and grade are we currently at?
2. From 1 June 2025, do we employ foreign Work Permit or S Pass construction workers, and is our registration aligned with the nation-wide registry expansion?
3. Do our current TR and PUC meet the current SRR minimums for our workhead and grade (the 7 June 2024 circular raised most entry-grade minimums)?
4. Do we hold SDCP (or, in CW, CCPP) for at least one technical person as our grade requires (A1 to B1 in CW; L6 in the graded CR workheads, and in ME01 to ME13 other than ME07 and ME13)?
5. For our current project portfolio, is our Public Liability limit at or above the limit each main contract sets?
6. For project CAR cover, are we verified as named Insured on each project's schedule, with cross-liability and waiver-of-subrogation clauses confirmed?
7. Does our BizSAFE or ISO 45001 certification meet what our CRS grade requires, and what main contractors ask for at onboarding?
