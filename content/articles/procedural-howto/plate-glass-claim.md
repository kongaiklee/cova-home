> **The Answer in 60 Seconds**
>
> Plate Glass cover can be included in a wider property or business package policy, or bought as a standalone policy, for example by businesses with significant glass exposure (storefronts, F&B, salons). The claim sequence: photograph the damage *before* clearing, secure the premises, notify the insurer within the policy window, get a quote from an approved glazier, and submit invoice + photos for reimbursement. The policy excess and any sub-limit on lettering, shelving glass, or signage typically apply. The damage cause matters: vandalism and accidental breakage are usually covered; gradual deterioration, scratching, and chipping are typically not.

### The Step-by-Step

A plate glass claim can go wrong when an SME treats the broken pane as an inconvenience and cleans up before documenting. By the time the insurer asks for evidence, the scene is gone.

#### Step 1 - Photograph the damage before doing anything else

The first action - before sweeping up, before calling the glazier, before anything - is photographs. From multiple angles. Wide shot showing the storefront context, mid-shot showing the full pane, close-up showing the break pattern.

The break pattern matters because it indicates cause:
- **Concentric rings around a point of impact** = projectile (stone, vandal)
- **A crack that starts at the edge at right angles to it** = typical of thermal stress, which most often breaks glass at an edge already damaged in handling or glazing
- **A meandering single or branching crack from the edge** = can be a low-stress mechanical break; the angle at which it leaves the edge helps tell it from a thermal crack
- **Toughened (tempered) glass shattered with no obvious impact point** = possibly spontaneous breakage, which BCA's Approved Document names as a risk of tempered glass (one cause is a nickel sulphide inclusion)

The cause decides the cover. A pre-existing fault may be excluded; a vandal attack is covered.

#### Step 2 - Secure the premises

A broken storefront pane is a security exposure. Once photographs are taken:

- Board up with plywood, tarp, or shrink-wrap to prevent further damage and theft
- If after-hours and you cannot board immediately, check whether your policy includes an emergency assistance service and, if it does, call it
- Note the time of damage discovery vs the time of boarding - this becomes part of the claim file

If a theft also occurred (smash-and-grab), do **not** clean up. Photograph the scene first, then call the police on 999, then file an SPF report. The Property/PAR or Burglary section of your policy may respond to the stolen contents alongside the Plate Glass cover for the broken pane.

#### Step 3 - Notify the insurer within the policy window

Notification terms vary by wording. Two Singapore examples:
- "Immediately", in writing: the wording in MSIG's SUMO SME package policy and in Etiqa's Plate Glass policy
- A written statement or particulars within a set number of days: 10 days in the SUMO conditions, 7 days in the Etiqa wording

Notify by phone first (24-hour hotline), follow up by email with photos and incident details. Include:
- Policy number
- Date and time of damage
- Location (specific storefront, internal partition, signage)
- Apparent cause
- Estimated repair cost (if known)
- Whether police were involved

#### Step 4 - Get glazier quotes

What the insurer asks for depends on the claim and the wording. Wordings such as MSIG's SUMO and Etiqa's Plate Glass policy oblige you to give the particulars and proof the insurer reasonably requires. For a larger claim it may ask for:
- 2 to 3 quotes from glazing contractors
- One from the insurer's panel (if available)
- Specification matching the original (laminated vs tempered, thickness, tinting, signage decals)

A reputable glazier should provide a written quote with breakdown by labour, glass material, hardware, signage replication, and warranty terms. BCA's Approved Document, explained in BCA's [Understanding the Approved Document](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/338/b7cdd6b6-a3c6-402d-8609-cfd84edf0094/understanding-the-approved-document-v3-0.pdf), requires laminated glass where glass forms part of a barrier, and glass complying with SS 341 (Specification for Safety Glazing Materials for Use in Buildings) in a facade, roof, canopy or overhead glazing at 2.4 metres or more, with protection such as screens or shields where tempered glass is used there.

#### Step 5 - Repair authorisation, then repair

Once the insurer authorises a quote, the glazier proceeds. The repair invoice should match the authorised quote unless variation has been pre-agreed.

Pay the glazier (or the insurer pays directly if cashless arrangement is in place), retain all receipts.

#### Step 6 - Submit the claim file

A complete Plate Glass claim file:
- Completed claim form
- Pre-repair photographs
- Police report (if vandalism, theft, or third-party action)
- Glazier quotes (the ones obtained for comparison)
- Authorised quote
- Final invoice
- Receipt of payment
- Any signage / decal reinstatement invoices

#### Step 7 - Settlement

The insurer pays:
- Authorised invoice amount
- Less the policy excess
- Subject to any limit stated in the schedule

For multi-pane events (storm damage, multiple-pane vandalism), aggregate sub-limits may apply. Read the policy schedule.

### What Plate Glass policies typically cover

- **Accidental breakage** of fixed glass at the insured premises
- **Malicious damage** by third parties (excluding the insured's own employees in some wordings)
- **Replacement cost** of the glass
- **Lettering, signage, decals** applied to the glass, only where the wording includes them: some treat insured glass as plain and exclude lettering unless the insurer agrees by endorsement
- **Boarding-up costs**, where the wording pays them; one Singapore plate glass policy excludes the cost of barricading
- **Damage to fixtures, fittings, or stock** caused by the breaking glass - sometimes included, sometimes carved out to PAR

### What Plate Glass policies typically exclude

- Gradual deterioration, weathering, discolouration
- Scratching, chipping, and superficial damage
- Damage during installation or removal
- Damage caused by the insured's own works (renovation contractors knocking the pane)
- Glass not permanently fixed (display cases, free-standing partitions - may be covered under contents instead)
- Pre-existing cracks
- Damage during alterations or extensions to the premises

### Common Mistakes / What Goes Wrong

1. **Cleaning up before photographing.** Insurer cannot verify cause; claim contested or reduced.
2. **Hiring a glazier and replacing the glass before notifying the insurer.** Some policies let the insurer choose to repair or replace the glass itself, and replacement done first means the insurer cannot verify the original damage.
3. **Treating the claim as too small to bother with.** Small Plate Glass claims (under S$2,000) accumulate over years and affect renewal pricing - but they're usually still claimable. Choose to absorb them as a deliberate decision, not by accident.
4. **Forgetting the signage/decal reinstatement.** The new glass is plain; the old glass had your business signage. Some policies cover signage replication; others don't. Check before finalising the quote.
5. **Confusing Plate Glass cover with Glass under PAR contents.** If the broken item is a glass display cabinet or interior partition, the claim may sit under PAR Contents, not Plate Glass.

### What This Means for Your Business

For F&B, retail, salon, and clinic operators with significant storefront glass:

1. **Confirm whether you have standalone Plate Glass cover or a Plate Glass section under PAR.** If you have a high-value storefront (designer shopfront, large display windows), standalone cover with higher limits may be more sensible than relying on a sub-limit.

2. **Train staff on the photograph-before-clean-up sequence.** Tape a short laminated card behind the counter: "Glass broken - STOP, photograph wide, mid, close, then secure, then call insurer."

3. **Keep the policy excess in mind for small claims.** If the excess is S$500 and the broken pane costs S$700 to replace, claiming may not be worth the renewal-rating impact.

4. **For high-traffic locations (Orchard Road, Bugis, Bukit Timah corner units), check vandalism cover specifically.** Some plate glass wordings exclude breakage caused by riot or civil commotion, or by the wilful act of your own staff.

The Plate Glass claim is operationally simple. The mistakes are operational, not technical. The discipline is photograph-first; everything else follows.

### Questions to Ask Your Adviser

1. Is my Plate Glass cover standalone or a sub-section of PAR/Fire? What is the sum insured per pane, per event, and aggregate per year?
2. Are signage, decals, and lettering covered, and what is the sub-limit?
3. Does the policy include damage to fixtures, fittings, and stock caused by breaking glass?
4. Is malicious damage by third parties covered, and does the policy exclude riot or civil commotion?
5. What is the excess, and at what claim size does the insurer require multiple quotes?
