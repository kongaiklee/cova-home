> **The 60-second answer:** BCA's ME workheads (ME01 to ME15) cover specialist mechanical and electrical work - air-conditioning, electrical engineering, fire protection, building automation, lifts and escalators, and more. Per the [BCA ME SRR (Aug 2026 Edition)](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/338/37a394e0-4e75-4958-aeda-bad07d944695/registration_me.pdf), some ME workheads require a licence (e.g., an IMDA telecommunication wiring contractor's licence for ME08 and ME15, and a PUB Plumber Licence or EMA Gas Service Worker Licence for ME12) - but BCA does not directly mandate insurance amounts. Insurance for ME firms typically comes from WICA, project tender requirements, and - for design-and-build M&E work - Professional Indemnity.

## The Sourced Detail

The ME Specific Registration Requirements ([ME SRR](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/338/37a394e0-4e75-4958-aeda-bad07d944695/registration_me.pdf)) define each ME workhead and the regulatory licences each requires. Examples:

- **ME01** - Air-Conditioning, Refrigeration & Ventilation Works
- **ME05** - Electrical Engineering. The ME SRR does not require a **Licensed Electrical Worker (LEW)**, but an EMA-licensed electrician can count as one of the required technical personnel for grades L1 and L2, and an EMA-licensed electrical technician or engineer for grades L3 to L5. Per the [Energy Market Authority](https://www.ema.gov.sg/regulations-licences/licences/worker-licences/electrical-engineers-licence), there are three classes of LEW: Electrician, Electrical Technician, and Electrical Engineer, with scope of work tied to voltage and approved load (45 kVA, 500 kVA, and above).
- **ME06** - Fire Prevention & Protection Systems
- **ME10** - Line Plant Cabling/Wiring for Telecommunications. The ME SRR sets no licence requirement for ME10; the IMDA telecommunication wiring contractor's licence is required for ME08 (Internal Wiring for Telecommunications) and ME15.
- **ME12** - Plumbing, Gas Piping & Sanitary Works. Per the ME SRR, applicants must hold "a valid PUB Plumber Licence or EMA Gas Service Worker Licence."
- **ME15** - Integrated Building Services. Per the ME SRR: "To be registered under this workhead, firms must already be registered at L2 and above under ME01, ME05 and at least one other ME workhead, which shall be ME02, ME04, ME06, ME08, ME11 or ME12."

ME workheads are graded L1 (entry) to L6 (highest). Per the [BCA tendering limits page](https://www1.bca.gov.sg/procurement/pre-tender-stage/contractors-registration-system-crs/crs-tendering-limits), L6 firms can enter public-sector tenders for specialist works of any value; lower grades have lower limits. The limits govern public-sector tenders; CRS registration itself is also required of any firm that hires construction S Pass or Work Permit holders.

### Insurance considerations specific to M&E

**Public Liability (PL) - heightened for fire/cabling work.** ME05 (Electrical) and ME06 (Fire Protection) firms work on systems where a single fault can cause catastrophic loss - e.g., a faulty cable termination causes a fire that destroys a tenanted floor. How a wording treats damage caused by faulty work matters: AIG's Singapore CGL wording, for example, excludes the cost of repairing the faulty work itself but not injury or damage resulting from it. Always ask for the policy exclusions in writing.

**Contractors All Risks / Erection All Risks.** For installation works (lifts, chillers, switchboards, fire panels), CAR or EAR insurance covers the works during installation and testing. The line between CAR (general construction) and EAR (machinery erection) matters because EAR is broader for testing and commissioning damage, a phase in which machinery breakdown and fire are main hazards.

**Professional Indemnity (PI).** ME contractors taking on design-and-build (e.g., a complete fire protection system designed and installed turnkey) face a "professional duty" exposure that CAR and PL do not pick up. PI responds to negligent design, specification errors, and shop-drawing mistakes that cause loss after handover. Per the [Professional Engineers Board "Common Queries" page](https://www1.peb.gov.sg/licensed-corporation/lc-common-queries/), a limited corporation licensed under section 30 of the Professional Engineers Act must be insured against professional liability; "the PE Act does not specify the amount to be insured", and the policy must comply with section 34 - you and your adviser set the limit based on your project exposure.

**Workmanship coverage.** A frequent gap: standard PL excludes the cost of redoing your own defective work.

**WICA.** Insurance is mandatory, per [MOM](https://www.mom.gov.sg/workplace-safety-and-health/work-injury-compensation), for all employees doing manual work and for non-manual employees whose salary, not counting overtime, bonuses, incentive payments and allowances, is S$2,600 a month or less, except in the classes the Work Injury Compensation (Insurance) Regulations 2020 exclude, such as employees of the Government, banks and retail-trade employers.

### Typical insurance stack for an ME contractor

- WICA - statutory.
- PL - annual programme, with a limit that meets the contracts you bid for.
- CAR / EAR - typically project-specific, sometimes annual for firms doing many small projects.
- PI - relevant if you take on design responsibility; the Professional Engineers Act requires it of a licensed limited corporation or limited liability partnership.
- Equipment transit cover for high-value equipment in transit / on-site.

## What This Means for Your Business

ME workhead grading sets the value of public-sector tenders your firm may enter, based on its technical expertise, track record and financial resources. Your insurance is about what happens when things go wrong - and in M&E, when things go wrong, they often go wrong expensively (fires, water damage, system outages).

Two practical traps worth flagging. First, the "subcontractor PI gap": if you're an ME05 firm subcontracting from a CW01 main contractor, your PL might not respond to claims arising from the main contractor's design - but the main contractor's PI might exclude your installation work. Negotiate the contractual allocation explicitly. Second, the "after handover" gap: most CAR policies end at practical completion or maintenance period expiry. Latent defects discovered later trigger PI (if you designed).

## Questions to Ask Your Adviser

1. My ME workhead is [X]. What policy structure do my main contractors typically require under their tender clauses?
2. Do I take on any design responsibility - even informally, through value-engineering proposals? If so, do I need PI?
3. Does my PL policy cover fire damage caused by my electrical work, or is there a sub-limit?
4. For lift/escalator/chiller installation, should I be on CAR or EAR? What's the practical difference for my biggest project?
5. If I'm a licensed PE corporation, what indemnity limit should my section 34 policy carry?
